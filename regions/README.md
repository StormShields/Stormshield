# Regions

A Region is one hand-written file, `regions/<name>/region.json`: the place's area, time zone, display units, official alert sources, the water it watches, the thresholds its flood-warning Rule set uses, its Shelters, High-ground points (each with its town's position), suggested sensor sites, the `hub_site` where its Hub runs (the Sandbox starts its Hub there) and the `sources` its Cache Pack is built from (`scripts/cache-pack/README.md`). The schema is `node/src/storm_shield/region.py`; a Hub (`NODE_REGION`, default `nepal`; a Sensor takes its Region from the Hub's pack) and the Cache Pack build both read the file through it, and a bad field is named in the error. Raw inputs sit beside it in `sources/`, and Events in `events/` (hand-written, a made-up one being `"real": false` with its weather written as a few keyframes; or generated from `sources/` like Fort Myers's `ian-2022.json`: `uv run --project node python scripts/pack_ian_event.py`); `scripts/build-cache-pack.sh <name>` turns the folder into the Region's Cache Pack. Spec: `docs/specs/regions-events-and-sandbox.md`, decision 2.

Thresholds are written with their units and converted where the Rule set reads them (`cm`, `m`, `ft`; `mm/h`, `in/h`; `hPa`, `inHg`).

The Region's `water` says what its Flood-Prone Area is. A `river` names the river line and the hand-drawn corridor files; a `coast` names the datum and `flood_prone_below`, and the build traces the land below that height from the terrain (`scripts/cache-pack/README.md`). A river Region's Shelters carry `height_above_river_m` and its rules a `flash_flood` block; a coast Region's Shelters carry neither and its rules a `storm_surge` block. `official_gauges` lists the tide gauges inside the box: the station's published level plus `datum_offset` is the height in the Region's datum. A Node on a coast Region refuses to start until the storm-surge Rule set is built.

## Nepal's thresholds

Tuned for a mountain river: the Bhote Koshi replay's calm water is 0.6 to 2.1 m and its calm pressure 1002 to 1008 hPa, so nothing below counts those as a flood. The 600 cm High level is the reported Syabrubesi warning level (danger 9 m); the Elevated level, the rise sizes and the pressure are reconstructed, as are the replay's own water values. The replay never goes below 1002 hPa: the 2026 flood was not a storm.

## Fort Myers's thresholds

Heights are the water surface above NAVD88 at the Sensor, in feet. The NWS/NHC storm-surge categories (the Potential Storm Surge Flooding Map: more than 1, 3, 6 and 9 ft of water above ground, <https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/surge/inundation/>) set them: Elevated at 1 ft, the Community Warning at 3 ft (the "life-threatening inundation" line of an NWS Storm Surge Warning), High at 6 ft, and the Flood-Prone Area is the land below 9 ft, the top category. The categories are depths above ground and these levels are heights above the datum, so they read the category as height above the datum: where the ground is higher than the datum the real depth is less, and a Sensor on the shore warns early rather than late. The 0.5 ft rise over 15 minutes, the rain levels (0.4 and 1 in/h, Nepal's 10 and 25 mm/h in inches) and 29.5 inHg (about 999 hPa) are placeholders, not NWS values. The one gauge in the box is NOAA 8725520; its recorded levels are feet above MHHW, and its datums page puts MHHW 0.28 ft above NAVD88 (5.68 ft against 5.40 ft on the station datum).

Its five Shelters are the schools and halls inside the box from the 14 shelters Lee County opened for Hurricane Ian (the county's own page refuses automated readers, so the list is from the City of Bonita Springs notice of 29 September 2022 and WGCU). Each `source` note holds the address, the OpenStreetMap position and the ground height read from the pack's terrain; none has been visited. Its sensor sites start from the USGS Ian deployment sites in Lee County inside the box (`sources/usgs-stn-ian-sensors-lee-collier.csv`) and run nearest the Fort Myers Beach Pier outwards.
